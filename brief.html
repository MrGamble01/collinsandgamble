The brief
builder.

The brief is the document scripts are usually trying to be — and they're worse at it. This is the five-section structure we send with every product on the agency side, made interactive so any brand can fill it in. Five answers. One printable brief. Yours to use with any creator, including ones we don't represent.

Reading the long version first? See why we won't approve a script and how we brief instead.

01

What the product is

One sentence in plain language. Not the marketing line — the functional description that would survive an editor's pass. The creator can write the marketing line themselves.

02

Who it's right for

The actual user, described by context, not demographic. "The person who keeps the kitchen clean themselves" beats "ages 25–54." This is what helps the creator know who they're speaking to.

03

The three features that actually matter

Out of all the things you could say, the three that drive the purchase. The creator decides which to lead with based on their feel for the audience.

04

What's true that's worth mentioning

Real claims, in plain language. Materials, origin, warranty, ship-from — anything that survives a fact-check. The creator decides whether and how to mention them.

05

What not to claim

Things the legal team would flag. Comparative performance numbers. Medical or safety claims that aren't substantiated. Anything the brand hasn't earned the right to say. The creator avoids these by knowing the boundary, not by reading around it.

06

Logistics & signoff (optional)

Optional fields that don't affect the brief's spine but help the creator if you include them.
