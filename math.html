The math,
estimated.

Four inputs in. A low / likely / high monthly range out. Plus the actual math we used to get there, expanded so you can argue with it. This estimates storefront commission only — brand deals are separate, larger, and impossible to model in a calculator. But the storefront half is where most creators leave money on the table, and it's where ranges actually mean something.

Why the storefront matters: read the long version · Program-wide context first? how creators actually earn.

01

Upload cadence

On-site review uploads per week. Cadence drives almost everything — Amazon rewards recency and the storefront only compounds when you keep showing up.

05101520
02

Audience reach

The audience that actually watches your reviews — not your follower count. Better to under-estimate. We use this to model how many shoppers land on a storefront link through your content in a month.

03

Primary category

Amazon's affiliate commission varies by category — some pay meaningfully better than others. We use the Amazon Associates rate schedule (which AIP commission tracks closely) as a baseline, applied to the category your storefront leans into.

04

Average price point

The typical price of items in your storefront. This drives per-click revenue more than anything except cadence — a $200-AOV storefront pays a lot more per click than a $15 one, even at lower commission rates.

Show our math

The model assembles four multipliers. Each one swings based on the inputs above.

  1. A Storefront clicks per month = uploads/week × 4.3 weeks × audience-engagement curve. The curve scales sub-linearly — a 100k creator gets roughly 4× the storefront clicks of a 10k creator, not 10×. (now: —)
  2. B Conversion to a purchased session = 3–8% of storefront clicks turn into at least one purchase in the same 24-hour window. Lower end = browse traffic; upper end = intent-heavy traffic from review-watchers. (now: 3–8%)
  3. C Session value = average product price × 1.4 (low) to 2.0 (high) — shoppers who arrive through a storefront link buy more than just the linked item. That multiplier is the storefront's main financial argument. (now: —)
  4. D Commission rate = Amazon Associates schedule for the category you picked. Most categories sit between 1% and 4%. Beauty and luxury beauty are the outliers. (now: —)

Monthly estimate = clicks × conversion × session value × commission. The low/high range runs the formula at the floor and ceiling of every multiplier together.

This is a model, not a guarantee. Amazon publishes rates but not personal performance. Variance between creators with identical inputs is significant — execution matters as much as inputs.

Where the money sits

The three revenue streams of the program, sized to scale by typical contribution. This calculator covers the first two. The third is what the agency actually unlocks — and it isn't model-able.

  • Storefront commission covered above — the steady, monthly part
  • On-site review bounties variable per-upload bonus — when Amazon's running it
  • Direct brand deals the largest, the most variable — not in this calculator
