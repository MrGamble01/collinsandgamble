What a creator manager
actually does.

Management is one of the vaguest words in the creator economy. Before you sign with anyone, including us, you should know what the job is supposed to be, and what it should never be.

Category · Practice Reading time · 6 minutes By · The agency Published · November 2026

Management is one of the vaguest words in the creator economy. Some managers run a creator's whole business. Others forward brand emails and take a cut. Before you sign with anyone, us included, you should know what the job is supposed to be.

The job, in one sentence

A creator manager runs the parts of your business that aren't filming, so you can film more and worry less.

For an Amazon Influencer, that means five things.

1. Sourcing

Finding brands whose products fit your category and your audience, then getting those products to your door with a brief. It does not mean asking you to buy products on spec and hope they earn.

2. Deals

Pricing your time. That covers flat fees, usage rights, exclusivity windows, and deliverables. A good manager knows what similar creators get paid and won't let you undercharge just because a brand asked nicely.

3. Storefront

Your storefront is a store. It needs categories, idea lists, a sensible layout, and current products. A manager should audit it, fix it, and keep it from going stale.

4. Coaching

Feedback on your actual videos: the hook, the length, what you show in the first five seconds, and whether you answered the question a shopper actually has.

5. Reporting

What earned and what didn't. Plain numbers, once a month, with one or two things to change.

What you keep

  • Your account. Your Amazon login, your payout account, your storefront. A manager works with the access you grant and loses it the day you revoke it.
  • Your opinion. Nobody should tell you what to think about a product.
  • Your content. Your videos are yours unless you explicitly license them in a deal.

Red flags

  • They want your password instead of delegated access.
  • They ask you to pay for products up front.
  • They promise brands a positive review. That puts your credibility, and your standing in the program, at risk.
  • They take a cut of your Amazon commissions with no clear service in return.
  • There's no written agreement, or no way out of it.
  • They can't explain their reporting in two minutes.

How we do it

We're paid by the brand side and through a commission on direct deals we negotiate for you. Amazon commissions go from Amazon to you. We don't script opinions, and we don't ask you to buy anything.

If that sounds like what you need, apply to the roster. Not sure you're ready? Take the check.
