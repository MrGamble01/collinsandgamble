How to read a brand deal
before you sign it.

Most bad brand deals aren't scams. They're vague. Here's what to check before a one-paragraph email turns into a commitment you didn't price.

Category · Contracts Reading time · 7 minutes By · The agency Published · October 2026

Most bad brand deals aren't scams. They're vague. A one-paragraph email turns into three revisions, a six-month exclusivity window, and a video that ends up running in paid ads you never priced. Here's what to check before you say yes.

Not legal advice. When the money is real, have someone qualified read the contract.

1. Deliverables: what exactly are you making?

Pin down:

  • How many videos, and in what format: on-site review, storefront video, or a social cut
  • The rough length
  • Where each one gets posted, and by when
  • How many rounds of changes, and what counts as a change. Fixing a wrong spec is fair. Rewriting your opinion isn't.

If it isn't written down, it isn't in the deal.

2. Rate: what is the work worth?

Your rate should reflect:

  • The time you spend filming and editing
  • Your audience and your conversion history in that category
  • Everything the brand gets beyond the video itself, including usage and exclusivity

A free product is not a rate. A product plus a fee is a deal.

3. Usage rights: where can they run your video?

This is the clause creators miss most often. Organic usage, meaning the brand can repost your video, is one thing. Paid usage, where your face runs in their ads, is something else, and it should cost more. Usage should have:

  • A channel list: their site, their social, paid ads
  • A time limit: 30, 90, or 180 days, never perpetual for free
  • A renewal price

4. Exclusivity: what are you agreeing not to do?

"No competing brands for 90 days" can quietly wipe out a whole category of your income. Ask:

  • Which competitors, exactly? Get a list or a narrow definition.
  • For how long?
  • Is the brand paying for that lost income?

5. Payment terms: when do you get paid?

  • Net 30 is normal. Net 90 is a loan you're giving the brand.
  • Is there a deposit?
  • What happens if they cancel after you've filmed? That's a kill fee.

6. Disclosure

The contract should never ask you to hide that the relationship exists. You disclose material connections every time, under the FTC's endorsement guides and Amazon's policies. If a brand pushes back on that, walk away.

The two-minute check

Before you sign, you should be able to answer all of these:

  1. What am I making?
  2. What am I paid, and when?
  3. Where can they use it, and for how long?
  4. What can't I do afterward?
  5. How do I disclose it?

If you can't, the deal isn't ready.
