Disclosure basics
for Amazon creators.

Disclosure isn't a box you tick to stay out of trouble. It's part of why the reviews work. Shoppers trust creators who are upfront about how they got the product.

Category · Compliance Reading time · 5 minutes By · The agency Published · August 2026

Disclosure isn't a box you tick to stay out of trouble. It's part of why your reviews work. Shoppers trust creators who are upfront about how they got the product.

This is a plain-language overview, not legal advice. The FTC's Endorsement Guides and Amazon's program policies are the sources to read in full, and both get updated.

What is a material connection?

It's any relationship a viewer might not expect that could affect how they weigh your opinion. Common ones:

  • You got the product for free
  • You were paid to make the video
  • You have a business relationship with the brand
  • You earn commission when someone buys

If any of those apply, disclose it.

Make it clear and early

  • Say it out loud in the video, not just in a caption.
  • Put it near the start, before the opinion.
  • Use plain words. "The brand sent me this for free" beats a hashtag nobody notices.

Don't let a brand buy your opinion

Disclosure and honesty go together. If a brand asks you to:

  • Only mention the positives
  • Read a script that states your opinion for you
  • Leave out a flaw you noticed

Say no. That's a red flag for the brand, and it puts your standing at risk too.

Brands: this protects you as well

Brands can be responsible for misleading endorsements made for them. Briefing creators to disclose clearly, and never scripting opinions, is the safest way to run a creator program.

How we handle it

Every brief we send includes a disclosure reminder. We don't pre-approve scripts. We won't work with creators who skip disclosure, or with brands who ask them to.
