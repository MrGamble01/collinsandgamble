A filming cadence
you can keep.

The creators who do well usually aren't the ones who post forty videos in a week. They're the ones still posting steadily a year later.

Category · Practice Reading time · 6 minutes By · The agency Published · September 2026

The creators who do well in the Amazon Influencer Program usually aren't the ones who post forty videos in one week. They're the ones still posting steadily a year later. Consistency compounds, and burnout resets you to zero.

Here's a cadence that holds up.

Batch by setup, not by product

Changing your light, background, and angle takes longer than filming. Group products by setup:

  • Kitchen day. Everything filmed on the counter.
  • Table day. Small electronics, beauty, accessories.
  • Room day. Furniture, decor, anything that needs space.

Three setups and one afternoon each can cover a week of uploads.

Pick a weekly number you can hit on a bad week

Not your best week. Your worst one. If you can always do five, commit to five. On a good week, bank the extras as drafts.

Keep a running "next to film" list

Every time a box arrives, it goes on the list with:

  • The date it arrived
  • Its category
  • Any deadline in the brief
  • A one-line note on what a shopper would actually want to know

When you sit down to film, you never start from a blank page.

The three-part video, every time

You don't need a new format for each product. You need one structure you can repeat:

  1. The answer up front. Who it's for, and whether you'd buy it again.
  2. The proof. Show the thing a shopper can't see in the photos: size in your hand, the texture, how it sounds, how it folds.
  3. The catch. Every product has one. Say it. That's why people trust you.

Build in a maintenance week

Once a month, film nothing new. Instead:

  • Fix storefront lists that went stale
  • Swap out discontinued products
  • Re-check that every video still matches the listing it's on

Protect the boring parts

Delegate sourcing, follow-ups, deal paperwork, and reporting. That's the work that drains your time without adding a single view. It's also exactly the work we take over for creators on our roster.
